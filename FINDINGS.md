# Interpretation of the Budhi Gandaki results

The ERA5 series covers January 1, 1955 through October 5, 2026. The reported
failure/flood date is October 11, 2026. Requested anomaly baseline: 1945–1975;
available reference observations: 1955–1975. No missing dates are reconstructed.

## Long-term changes

Complete-year Mann–Kendall and Sen results cover 1955–2025. Significant trends
under the original test and within-table Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment include:

| Variable | Sen change per decade | Adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual mean air temperature | +0.305 °C | <0.001 |
| Annual rainfall total | +16.2 mm | <0.001 |
| Rainfall percentage of total precipitation | +1.12 percentage points | 0.00076 |
| Annual mean snow water-equivalent depth | −7.4 mm | 0.00040 |
| Annual precipitation total | +13.6 mm | 0.044 |
| Annual snowmelt total | −22.3 mm | 0.038 |

Rainfall and snowfall shares are complementary and are not independent lines
of evidence. Annual freeze–thaw frequency did not increase significantly.
Original Mann–Kendall p-values and Sen confidence intervals assume independence;
serial dependence can overstate significance. Trend significance is not evidence
that a variable caused the October failure.

## Candidate antecedent conditions

- September 24–27 totals approximately 161.2 mm precipitation, including
  154.5 mm water-equivalent snowfall and 6.7 mm rainfall at this ERA5 point.
  The snowfall exceeds all September four-day windows in the available
  1955–1975 baseline; this is an empirical comparison, not a formal significance test.
- Modeled snow water equivalent rises from approximately 2 mm on September 23
  to 147 mm on September 28. These are water-equivalent depths, not physical snow thickness.
- The 15-day topsoil-moisture mean ending October 5 is approximately
  0.396 m³/m³, around the 96th percentile of October baseline windows.
- The seven days ending October 5 have approximately 0.27 mm precipitation.
  Mean air temperature is −3.82 °C, around the 55th percentile, and snowmelt
  is around the 73rd percentile of corresponding October baseline windows.

Long-term warming, recent snow accumulation and elevated modeled wetness are
hypotheses for contributing conditions, not established immediate triggers.
The storm ends 14 days before failure and October 6–11 weather is absent.
ERA5 soil temperature and moisture do not measure rock-fracture conditions,
permafrost degradation, glacier basal water pressure or glacier mass balance.

## Priority follow-up investigations

1. Map the actual release area and verify whether the ERA5 point represents its elevation and aspect.
2. Obtain October 6–11 hourly weather and reconstruct loading, temperature and melt conditions.
3. Examine spatial snowfall, wind redistribution and snow retention.
4. Evaluate glacier thinning, crevasses and pre-failure slope displacement using satellite observations.
5. Investigate source-slope geology, ice-filled fractures and water-pressure pathways.
6. Check sensitivity to serial dependence and alternative anomaly baselines.
